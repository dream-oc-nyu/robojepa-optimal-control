# E009 — do the two native failures belong to the planner or to the guard?

Arm P3 (action interface), the first missing control of the E006 and E008 verdicts (review, 2026-11-13). Written 2026-11-13 20:50Z, with the 8 native units, the 8 units of the 5 cm cap and both E008 replays read, with `cem_cap10` (E007) running and unread, and before any guard-off unit.

## Question
Native CEM fails on instances 100 and 107. E008: both end frozen by the release wrapper's joint-margin guard (position command zeroed while any arm joint is within 0.15 rad of a limit), entered at the end of a 22.4 and a 20.4 cm executed move, after the arm had already overshot the goal (100: 7.3 → 11.6 → 10.8 → 12.3 cm; 107: 7.1 → 14.1 → 9.7 cm). With the guard disabled and nothing else changed, does native CEM recover within the 5 ticks?

## Why
The dev gain of the 5 cm cap (mean progress 0.587 → 0.888) is carried by these two instances alone; on the other six the cap costs 0.081. If native CEM recovers without the guard, that gain is avoidance of a controller rule that any shorter step would give, and reach-v0's failure count says nothing about the planner. If it does not, the overshoot on near starts is a failure of the action interface itself.

What the logs say about recovery. Outside the 5 cm cube the model fixes a component's sign and the sampler its length, 9.0–21.7 cm (median 14.3) in the baseline. A single saturated axis with `|r|` cm remaining lands inside the cube when the drawn length falls in `(|r| − 5, |r| + 5)`. For `|r|` ≈ 10–12 cm and lengths spread over 9–22 cm that is roughly 0.6 per tick; once inside, distance contracts by 0.53 per tick. Instance 100 has two ticks left after its entry tick (from 12.3 cm), 107 has three (from 9.7 cm). Among the native successes, 106 stood 12.1 cm away with two ticks left and finished at 1.8 cm; 102, 103 and 105 stood 9.2–11.5 cm away with three ticks left and finished at 0.5–1.4 cm; all four are selected on success.

## Arm

The change is a wrapper around the environment in a new driver, as in E008; `bench/` is not edited. Per substep the driver records the minimum joint margin and the position command, as E008 did. Units are written under `runs/E009-guard-off/` and are not reach-v0 units: they are never scored into `bench/leaderboard.jsonl`. Two units, about 37 minutes of GPU.

Not in this episode: a guard-off run of the capped arms (their replays show no guard activity) or of the six other instances (no guard activity in the native replay).

## Prediction (before any guard-off unit)
1. *Same computation up to the entry.* Distances before the entry tick are within 1.5 cm of the native run's (100: after ticks 0 and 1; 107: after tick 0). Same-seed native reruns differ at the mm level on saturated axes, so the later ticks are not expected to repeat.
2. *The arm keeps moving.* No tick executes less than 1 mm on a command of at least 1 cm (native: 2 and 3 such ticks), and the minimum margin falls below 0.15 rad on both instances.
3. *Partial recovery.* Both end closer than the guarded finals (12.32 and 9.69 cm); at least one of the two ends within 5 cm; at most one ends within 2 cm. Progress is positive on at least one.
4. *The 5 cm cap still wins on these two.* Both end farther than under the cap (0.42 and 0.77 cm).

## What would change the plan
- Both end within 2 cm → the native failures are the guard's. The cap's dev gain is then avoidance of a wrapper rule, the interface arm has no gain left on reach-v0 beyond that, and the hill-climb turns to what remains for every arm: 212 s of planning per tick for a 3-dimensional search, and the 0.2–1.8 cm residual (P1, P2, P5 inside the cube at matched and reduced time). Blocker 2 is answered in part: the failure count is an environment property and should be reported as such, whatever the headline.
- Neither ends closer than its start (7.29, 7.07 cm) → near starts fail by overshoot without any guard: the cap's gain on them is the interface's, and E007's dose–response decides the cap.
- In between (prediction 3) → both contribute; report the guard entries and the overshoot separately, and add near and low-margin starts to the dev set as its own evaluator episode before any claim.
- 2 fails because the arm stalls at a physical joint limit → the guard was hiding a real limit; the units are read as one-sided stalls and the question moves to executed step length against margin, per arm.
- 1 fails → the runtime wrapper changed more than the guard; fix before reading anything else.
